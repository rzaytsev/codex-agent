# Solfege and musical interpretation

An interval compares two pitches, not two peaks in a spectrum. A single sung
note has harmonics; its second harmonic alone does not establish a second singer
an octave above. Voice separation, pitch tracking and identifying singer/part
are different tasks. Track continuity, onsets and the original recording before
assigning a part. With three or more notes, say which pair defines the interval.

| 12 | Octave | Чистая октава |

These are equal-tempered distances. At A4=440 Hz, C4–E4 is a major third,
C4–Eb4 a minor third, C4–F4 a fourth, C4–G4 a fifth and C4–C5 an octave.
Measured deviations in cents are relative to the stated tuning reference, not
automatically a performance error. Expressive singing, ensemble tuning, just
intonation and intentional slides need musical judgment.

The helper labels pitch classes with sharps. Enharmonically equal pitches can
have different written interval names: C–D# and C–Eb have the same equal-tempered
distance but different diatonic spelling. Use the actual key/score to explain
notation; do not claim that MIDI uniquely determines spelling. In Russian use
До, Ре, Ми, Фа, Соль, Ля, Си when useful; clarify fixed-do versus movable-do only
if the lesson depends on it. Always specify an octave for an executable example.

## Constructing a second voice in a demo

1. Establish the target phrase, actual lead line, accompaniment/chords and usable
   singer range. State tentative key/chord hypotheses as tentative.
2. Choose an interval or musical function: upper/lower third, fifth, octave,
   held chord tone or a moving independent line. A fixed fourth/fifth can sound
   deliberately exposed or stylistically specific; there is no universal best
   interval without the musical context and desired effect.
3. For diatonic thirds, choose every second scale degree, not a constant
   semitone shift. In C major: C–E is major, D–F minor, E–G minor, F–A major.
4. Check each harmony note against the current chord and nearby melody notes.
   Attend to voice crossings, large jumps, clashes, phrase endings and intended
   tensions. A scale-only guide cannot make those decisions automatically.
5. Audition the second voice alone, with a synthesized melody and with the
   unchanged accompaniment when an appropriate original stem is available.
   Keep generated guides separate and label them as proposed choices.

For a choir, accurate per-part work normally benefits from original stems or a
score. If only a stereo mix exists, return candidate pitch combinations and
bounded examples. Do not force every moment into third/fourth/fifth/octave:
other intervals, silence and unresolved overlap are valid outcomes.

Visuals are selective. A piano roll shows when pitches overlap; a pitch curve
shows tuning and slides. Neither certifies transcription. Staff notation needs
additional rhythmic quantization, spelling and engraving and is outside the
current helper. Never replace low-confidence data with precise-looking notation.
